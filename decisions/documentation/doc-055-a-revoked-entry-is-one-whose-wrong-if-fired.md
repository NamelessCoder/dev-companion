# D-DOC-055 — A revoked entry is one whose Wrong if fired

**An entry whose queued work landed keeps the status its reading left and gains
a **Since then**; `revoked` stays what a **Wrong if** that fired leaves.**

Nine entries were in one situation — the gap they named is filled — and were
closed out two ways, so a listing showed four carried-out decisions among the
dead ends.

## Evidence

- The rule is already written.
  [`decisions.rst`](../../documentation/records/decisions.rst) says the status
  names the last dated section, that a reading settling the **Wrong if** changes
  it, and that a reading settling neither leaves it and adds a **Since then**.
- Four entries carried a `Revoked on` written under a `Confirmed on` whose
  **Wrong if** had been read and had not fired: `D-KNW-072`, `D-KNW-074`,
  `D-KNW-103` and `D-ANS-029`. Three of them open that section with the same
  sentence — "By the work this entry queued".
- Five entries in the same situation stayed confirmed: `D-KNW-027`, `D-KNW-065`,
  `D-KNW-076`, `D-KNW-099` and `D-KNW-100`. `D-KNW-076`'s reading says it
  outright — "Neither **Wrong if** fired, and that is the finding".
- Both practices ran on the same two days, 2026-08-14 and 2026-08-18, so this
  was two answers to one question rather than a practice that moved.
- `D-KNW-040` is the revocation the rule keeps. Its first **Wrong if** is what
  happened, read on 2026-08-03, and the entry says which.
- What it cost is where the listing puts them.
  [`writing-a-decision.rst`](../../documentation/records/writing-a-decision.rst)
  keeps the revoked entries in a run of their own "because mixed into the rest
  they looked exactly like something to build on", so the four read as dead ends
  and the reading each carries was filed as withdrawn.

## Decided

- The four are corrected to `confirmed` and their `revokedBy` is dropped. What
  the reading found stands; what the work did is not a reason to withdraw it.
- Where the `Confirmed on` already named the successor, the `Revoked on` section
  goes — `D-KNW-072` names `D-KNW-073` in the reading itself, so converting it
  would say one thing twice. Where only the revocation carried the pointer, it
  becomes a **Since then** and keeps it.
- A **Since then** is undated, so `Decisions::datedLines()` does not read it and
  the status keeps naming the `Confirmed on` above it. No check changes.
- **Where a revoked entry has a part that still holds, a decision covering that
  part is what carries it, and `revokedBy` names it.** `D-DIS-002` left the DDEV
  half to the queue and `D-DIS-007` took it; `D-KNW-001`'s domain half was
  answered by `D-KNW-006`. Both were that pattern with the link missing, and
  both now name their successor. This is the second half of what
  [`D-DOC-003`](doc-003-a-decision-says-what-came-back.md) settled: it weighed
  an `amended` state for the partly-wrong entries and dropped it, because a
  state saying "some of this is safe" does not say which. What it left the
  reader is the revocation's own dated line, and what this adds is the entry
  that carries the half still standing.
- Not a check. Whether a **Wrong if** fired is a reading, and nothing here can
  hold a dated section's prose to one. What keeps this is the session writing
  the section asking which of the two it is.
- The concern the four raised is real and the title is where it is answered: a
  confirmed entry saying "X is missing from the corpus" reads as a live gap,
  which is why the family it belongs to was retitled — `D-DOC-056`.

## Assumed

- That the four readings were as complete as their `Confirmed on` says. Each was
  reread here for whether a **Wrong if** fired, not repeated against the
  checkouts.
- That no entry needs a third state for "carried out". Nine were enough to see
  the split and none of them wanted a word `confirmed` does not have.

## Wrong if

- A reader takes a confirmed entry whose statement says "X is missing from the
  corpus" for a live gap and writes X a second time. The state would then be
  carrying a claim the title was supposed to carry, and the title sweep would
  not have been enough.
- A revoked entry turns up whose `Revoked on` names a part that still holds and
  no decision carries it. Four do today — `D-CAT-002`, `D-KNW-002`, `D-SCO-001`
  and `D-SCO-004` — and `todo/open/2026-08-23-120000` is what reads them; if the
  reading finds no decision is owed after all, this bullet is where the rule is
  too strict.
- A session writes a `Revoked on` for work it carried out anyway. The rule would
  then be one nobody applies at the moment it is needed, and a report over the
  dated sections is the lever left.
