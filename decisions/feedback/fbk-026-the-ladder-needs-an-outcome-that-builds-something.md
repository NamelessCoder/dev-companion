# D-FBK-026 — The ladder needs an outcome that builds something

**A judgement that a capability is missing decides that it is built, and *taken
on* is the answer it gets.**

The six answers a feedback could receive were: fix a sentence, move a rule, add
a routing line, note that it was already answered, trim it, queue it, or propose
not doing it. None of them says *this gets built*, and the channel exists to
decide exactly that.

## Evidence

- The page opened by saying a feedback "is *not* a work item" and closed with
  six answers, four autonomous. The two that could carry a new capability hedge
  it: step 1b ended "Both are queued rather than closed on the spot: a tool and
  a skill are contracts", and *Queued* described the change as "too large for
  the spot", which is a size rather than a kind.
- The ladder's preamble named step 1 as the guess a lazy judgement makes — "the
  guess is always step one, which is why gaps get a fourth entry written next to
  three that already exist". Read as written, the most expensive rung is the
  suspect one and every cheaper answer is the diligent one.
- What that produced is measurable and is in `D-FBK-025`: 44 of 48 cards at
  `low`, 30 of them unjudged, and 35 open feedback from one checkout describing
  two skills nobody had decided to build.
- The corpus is full of feature-shaped reports that no rung welcomes: an
  API-stability lookup (`feedback/2026-08-01-115713`), a changelog index that
  reaches method names rather than titles (`115112`), a change-scope tool that
  hands `typo3_test_run_guide` the paths this server insists the caller
  determines (`122326`), Forge and Gerrit reads (`2026-08-02-144511`, `145217`,
  `145230`). Each was filed by a session that had just paid for its absence.

## Decided

- A seventh answer, **taken on**, and it is autonomous: `decisions/` records
  that the thing is built, what its boundary is and what would falsify the
  boundary; the card carries the first step at a priority the judgement sets;
  the feedback stays open until the commit that ships it archives it. What waits
  is what nobody here can establish — what the tool answers about TYPO3, what
  the skill says — and that is research rather than a question upward.
- *Queued* is repair of something that exists; *taken on* is a capability that
  does not. The old wording made them one rung apart in size.
- What justifies building is the corpus rather than the ask. Two sessions
  arriving at the same shape from different tasks is the evidence; one
  session's suggestion is the weakest thing in the file, because its author was
  guessing about this repository exactly as much as a judging run guesses about
  TYPO3.
- *Proposed* now has to say what building would have cost and what the corpus
  behind it weighs. It is the outcome that looks like diligence from every
  angle, and it was the cheapest one to reach.

## Assumed

- That the bias was in the page rather than in the sessions reading it. The
  judgements it produced are the evidence, and they are all one way.
- That deciding before the reading is safe for a shape and not for a fact. A
  tool's existence is established here; what it answers is not.

## Wrong if

- Something is built that the reading afterwards shows was the wrong shape —
  a tool whose verb is wrong, a skill covering half a domain. Then the decision
  needed the research in front of it and this entry moved it behind.
- The corpus stops distinguishing anything, because every feedback becomes a
  feature. The signal is *taken on* being reached with one session behind it
  more than once.
