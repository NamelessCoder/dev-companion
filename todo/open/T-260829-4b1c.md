# Take the draft status out of the skills and revoke what put it there

A skill declaring itself a draft is published to nobody unless a run asks for
it, and the questions it exists to raise are the ones a reader answers anyway:
a skill that does not work is corrected where it is read, or the fix is shipped.
It also makes testing the publication harder, and a release number will carry
what it carries. Nothing declares itself one today, so nothing is stranded by
the removal. Take out `Installer::DRAFT`, `DRAFT_KEY`, `drafts()`,
`publishSet()`'s parameter, the `drafts` key in the record and the `--drafts`
argument in `Server\Entrypoint`, making `digest()` take no parameter; drop
`InstallerTest::aDraftIsPublishedOnlyWhereTheRunAsksForIt` and the two draft
tests in `SkillTest`, keeping `everyFrontMatterFieldIsOneTheStandardDefines`,
which holds the closed field set and is the half worth keeping. Then revoke
`D-SKL-027` in place with an entry saying what replaced it — `D-DOC-052` moves
its `#[Decision]` attributes to the revoking entry — and rewrite the prose that
names the mechanism: `documentation/usage/installing.rst` ("Trying a draft"),
`documentation/usage/task-skills/readme.rst`, the review step in
`documentation/contributing/writing-a-skill.rst`, and the sentences in
`D-DIS-013`, `D-DIS-021`, `D-SKL-021`, `D-SKL-054` and `R-DIS-025`. Every
recorded digest changes with the signature, so every project reads as stale once
and the refresh `D-DIS-021` added puts it right on the next session.
